# Volume III — Private Field Equipment

The red volume disliked the word *device*.

Seris discovered this when its title changed beneath her hand.

**FIELD DEVICES FOR PRIVATE VOLUNTARY RESTRAINT** became **FIELD EQUIPMENT**, then settled into:

**PRIVATE FIELD EQUIPMENT: INSTRUMENTS RECOVERED, ADAPTED OR IMPROPERLY TESTED BY ZEPHIRA OF THE ASKARI**

*Z.: Remove “improperly.”*

*E.: Four weeks instead of four hours.*

*Z.: The selector markings were worn.*

*C.: They were written in three languages and one of them was upside down.*

*Z.: Thank you.*

*C.: I did not say you read any of them.*

Seris smiled despite herself and turned the page.

Emily's preface distinguished private self-bondage from performance. The subject was simultaneously designer and participant, giver and receiver, the person establishing the predicament and the person required to endure it. Its peculiar intimacy came not from an audience but from foreknowledge. A woman built her own helplessness while fully capable of walking away, then crossed a threshold after which the earlier version of herself held authority over the later one.

**The lock is a message sent forward in time,** Emily wrote. **The key says: I knew you would reconsider. I chose not to trust you.**

Zephira had underlined the sentence.

There was no joke beside it.

## The First Restraint

Emily did not repeat the full account of Zephira's first discovery. She recorded the provenance: a Lut Gholein trader, a sealed Askari coffer, a restraint woven with responsive filaments and sold by a shopkeeper who insisted it was ceremonial because admitting ignorance would have lowered the price.

Zephira bought it because it looked useful.

She tested it because it looked pretty.

The catalogue did not pretend those were equally important reasons.

Its chief virtue was anatomical intelligence. It understood the difference between pressure and injury, between a tight line that framed an athletic, soft body and a constriction that threatened circulation. It could arrange a compact hogtie, a frog tie with wrists secured behind the small of the back, a forward-leaning strappado or a four-point bar that left no comfortable position without inventing impossible joints.

Emily's diagrams were meticulous. Every wrist, ankle, elbow and attachment point was accounted for. Dotted lines showed the small range of motion remaining. Notes identified which movements merely redistributed pressure and which could actually alter the tie.

Seris appreciated that precision more than she wanted to admit.

The restraint's appeal to Zephira had not required philosophy. She was a hardened traveler, a gambler, a drinker and an Amazon accustomed to occupying space without apology. In the restraint she looked—by her own later assessment—soft, helpless and embarrassingly cute. The contradiction excited her. She did not need to become delicate in daily life to enjoy an evening in which the rope made delicacy visible.

*Z.: “Cute” was Cordelia's word.*

*C.: You agreed.*

*Z.: I was gagged.*

*E.: You nodded.*

*Z.: Under hostile questioning.*

*C.: The question was “Are you comfortable?”*

The volume displayed the relevant incident as a sidebar. Zephira had selected four weeks instead of four hours and, being incapable of reaching the controls, had remained bound until Cordelia and Emily happened upon her. The ball gag prevented explanation. Cordelia therefore asked yes-or-no questions: injury, panic, consent to examination, consent to touch. Zephira shook her head twice, nodded once after a conspicuously long pause, and discovered that being admired while helpless affected her more deeply than being helpless alone.

They left when she asked to finish in private.

That departure mattered as much as the discovery. Zephira learned that sharing knowledge of her predicament need not surrender ownership of it. Cordelia learned that helping could end with leaving. Emily learned nothing, according to her own footnote, because she had been correct already.

## The Appraising Glass

The Glass had been recovered from a ruined bath suite beneath the Lost City. It possessed no reflective backing. Instead it constructed a local image from several angles at once, allowing a restrained user to see what an ordinary mirror and an immobile neck would hide.

It did not record. It did not transmit. It could not display anyone outside the room.

Cordelia had apparently made Emily inscribe those limitations on the frame.

*C.: Privacy should not depend upon trusting the inventor's mood.*

*E.: An excellent principle. Also hurtful.*

The Glass was factual, which made it crueler than flattery. It showed the compression of rope against soft curves, the helpless arch of a bound body, the flushed evidence of arousal and the precise moment composure failed. In its ordinary mode it added neutral annotations: **CIRCULATION NORMAL**, **LEFT ANKLE ROTATION WITHIN SAFE RANGE**, **SUBJECT HAS CEASED PRETENDING POSITION IS MERELY TECHNICAL**.

Zephira objected to the third caption.

The Glass replied in a footnote of its own.

*A.G.: Objection did not alter observable conditions.*

Seris covered her mouth with one hand. Her laugh sounded scandalously loud in the empty room.

She understood the Glass immediately. A person alone could imagine that her expression remained stern, that her body's response was subtle, that the ropes looked functional rather than beautiful. The Glass removed imagination as a defense. It showed the angle she most wanted to see and most wanted hidden.

Its deepest intimacy was not exposure to another person. It was exposure to oneself.

Seris found herself studying a diagram of Zephira in a compact tie, not because the rendering was salacious—it was no more explicit than an anatomical plate—but because a tiny caption beside the subject's face read:

**ATTEMPTED SCOWL IS NOT CONVINCING.**

*Z.: Machines should not have opinions.*

*E.: Correct. That is why the caption is factual.*

## The Resonant Pearl

The Pearl looked harmless enough to be jewelry and cost enough to be a weapon.

It had been designed for conditional-release games. The user selected stakes while calm: a probability of release, a consequence for failure, and a limit beyond which the Pearl could not bargain. Once arousal compromised judgment, the calm agreement remained authoritative.

Emily devoted six pages to probability tables.

Zephira had crossed out four.

*Z.: No one reads a probability table while tied up.*

*E.: That is why one reads it before.*

*C.: I read them during.*

*Z.: You also farm Lower Kurast for relaxation.*

The Pearl's games were elegantly mean. One choice offered even odds of immediate release; failure added an hour and tightened the posture. Another offered a one-in-four chance but required the user to choose before seeing which of four covered runes represented mercy. A third allowed the wearer to surrender the wager, but surrender produced an exposing adjustment and a written receipt naming exactly what she had given up to avoid the risk.

Zephira liked the gamble because the uncertainty survived familiarity. She could know every mechanism and still not know what would happen when the rune turned. Her confidence as a gambler collided with the bodily desperation of a restrained woman who wanted one outcome too much. Loss became humiliating because she had chosen it; victory became intoxicating because it had never been owed.

Seris traced a probability tree with one finger.

“Terrible expected value,” she whispered.

The page produced a line of green script.

**CORRECT.**

Then, beneath it:

**WOULD READER STILL TAKE THE WAGER?**

Seris withdrew her hand.

After a moment she placed it back.

“Not without defining the failure state.”

**PROMISING.**

## Courteous Silence

The magical ball gag's formal name had been intended as a joke. Emily denied this twice, which confirmed it.

Courteous Silence protected breathing, adjusted fit and left the wearer able to answer prearranged yes-or-no questions by moving her head. It did not connect to, operate or share an origin with Cordelia's separately supplied calling charm. It could muffle speech without erasing communication. In its ordinary state it was an unusually considerate tool.

It also listened.

Not for secrets. Not for words. For involuntary sound.

Its blue inset warmed when the wearer lost control of her voice: the first muffled gasp when a restraint settled, a frustrated noise when the Pearl rejected a wager, the small betrayed moan drawn out by a vibrator moving according to a program the subject herself had selected.

Zephira had initially regarded those sounds as failures of discipline. The artifact classified them as useful feedback.

**A vocal response is not evidence that the subject has lost herself,** Emily wrote. **It is evidence that the body has stopped wasting effort on a lie.**

The vibrator received only a short appendix. It was magical, adaptable and capable of attaching to a restraint without occupying the user's bound hands. Its patterns could be controlled by time, motion, breath, the Pearl's wagers or the Glass's observations. It had no personality. Emily emphasized this as if expecting praise.

*Z.: The absence of commentary is its finest quality.*

*C.: It pulses smugly.*

*E.: That is projection.*

*C.: You built it.*

*E.: Well-supported projection.*

Seris read the appendix twice. On the second reading, the volume quietly offered a folded technical plate showing the control runes. She unfolded it, then became aware of the warmth in her face and the pressure of her knees together beneath the archive table.

No chair measured her. No Glass displayed her. The privacy made honesty possible.

## Non-Vohl Appendix: The Calling Charm

The calling charm was not a Vohl artifact.

Cordelia removed the plain disk of blue glass from her own Horadric Cube after she and Emily discovered Zephira in the Lost City bath. Emily included it only because it became an important companion to Zephira's private field equipment. It was not part of the recovered suite, possessed no Black Rose setting and owed nothing to Emily's design.

It was also not a watcher.

Emily repeated that point in capital letters.

It did not report Zephira's location, posture, arousal or activity. It did not activate merely because a session lasted a long time. Death in Sanctuary was recoverable; hunger, thirst and four weeks of cramping were more practical concerns. The charm remained inert unless Zephira sincerely tried to communicate outward to anyone at all. Only then did it deliver the call and a location.

Zephira accepted it after her mistaken four-week setting. Not because she wished to be observed, but because being able to choose contact did not make the solitude less real.

Later she permitted Cordelia and Emily to know what she was planning. Later still, Cordelia joined her. Two women designed their own helplessness side by side, each responsible for the architecture and each delighted when the earlier plan became inconvenient to the woman living inside it.

That was the progression Emily considered important: isolation, discovery, privacy respected, help requested, companionship chosen.

Not rescue as romance. Not supervision disguised as safety. Trust as the right to decide when another person entered the room.

## Notes on the Subject

The last section belonged mostly to Zephira.

She wrote that self-bondage did not expose a hidden, delicate identity. She remained the same woman who drank sailors under tables, lost money with confidence, hunted demons and mocked expensive equipment. The softness was not truer than the armor. It was simply another truth.

Rope gave it a shape.

The Glass allowed her to see it. The gag allowed her to hear it. The Pearl made her gamble against it. Cordelia and Emily, once invited, allowed her to share it without turning it into theirs.

*Z.: That is the only paragraph in this volume I approve without correction.*

*E.: I wrote it.*

*Z.: Then I withdraw the statement.*

*C.: Too late. Archival ink.*

Seris rested both hands on the open book.

Of the three volumes, this one felt closest to her. Not because she imagined herself an Amazon, or brave enough to misread a four-hour dial into four weeks, but because the beginning required no audience. Curiosity could remain private. A woman could learn what moved her without turning herself into a public mystery.

And if, one day, she chose to let someone know—there were charms for that too.

The final page displayed a catalogue note.

**All Vohl instruments catalogued in this volume retain dormant Black Rose settings. Cordelia's calling charm is expressly excluded. These settings are neither accidental nor suitable for unannounced activation. See Volume IV.**

Beneath it, Zephira had written:

*Do not read Volume IV while angry with Emily.*

Cordelia had replied:

*This excludes every day ending in “day.”*

Emily's answer occupied the bottom margin.

*Read it aroused instead. It improves peer review.*

Seris looked at the silver volume.

Then she extinguished one candle, checked the archive door, and opened it.
