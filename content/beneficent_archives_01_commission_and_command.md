# The Beneficent Archives

## I. Commission and Command

Mira found Charsi’s diary beneath requisitions for tent canvas.

At twenty-five, she had already learned that the Monastery archives rewarded patience more reliably than intelligence. Intelligence encouraged a person to search where important records ought to have been. Patience eventually revealed that the first inventory of rescued Rogue history had classified Pepin’s medical correspondence as military intelligence and stored Kashya’s original campaign maps in a barrel labeled **DRIED APPLES — DO NOT OPEN**.

The barrel had contained no apples.

The chamber beneath the rebuilt east cloister was cool, windowless and full of objects which had survived demons only to become paperwork. Mira sat cross-legged between two open crates, her dark-red hair pinned away from her face and her short Rogue bow resting against the wall. Dust softened the knees of her trousers. She had been assigned six shelves before supper and completed two.

The leather volume beneath the canvas orders bore Charsi’s smith mark: a small hammer surrounded by six sparks. Its first pages recorded horseshoes, arrowheads and repairs to the outer gate. Twenty years had faded the ink to brown without diminishing the warmth of the writer’s voice.

*Cordelia returned today. She looks much the same, except she has become better at pretending her commissions are ordinary.*

Mira stopped.

Cordelia of the Zann Esu appeared in histories, songs and one mural which made her shoulders too broad and her clothing implausibly respectable. The Sorceress had defeated the Prime Evils, survived the breaking of Mount Arreat and spent the following decades pursuing every unique and set item known to Sanctuary. Children played at being Cordelia. Apprentices complained that their instructors invoked her whenever someone objected to difficult terrain.

Mira had never encountered a reference to her commissioning work from Charsi.

She turned the page.

*She requires two steel cuffs lined with silk. They must lock behind the wearer’s back, resist teleportation and support her full weight without bruising the wrists.*

*I asked whether she was designing equipment for a prisoner.*

*She said, “Temporarily.”*

*I asked who had captured the prisoner.*

*She became interested in the forge chimney.*

Mira smiled. The next three pages contained measurements, hinge sketches and a careful study of load distribution. Charsi had tested the completed bracers with sacks of iron heavier than any woman. She had rounded every edge, recessed the locks and hidden a quick release where only someone standing behind the wearer could reach it.

The final line had been underlined.

*Cordelia requested that pulling against the cuffs make them tighter. I explained that this is ordinarily considered a flaw.*

*She said ordinary equipment had never been her problem.*

A slip of paper protruded from the binding. Mira drew it free and discovered a second hand: angular, disciplined and instantly recognizable from the orders preserved in the Rogue command archive.

Kashya.

The paper was headed **FIELD EVALUATION: REARWARD BRACERS**.

Mira read it once as a military document. Halfway through, that interpretation became impossible.

*Location: eastern chamber, Monastery jail.*

*Participants: Cordelia and myself.*

*Agreed release word: Tristram.*

*The cuffs held Cordelia’s wrists securely behind her during standing restraint and partial suspension. Pulling against them produced the intended tightening response. She tested this repeatedly after being instructed not to.*

*At twenty-six minutes, she requested release without using the agreed word. The request was denied.*

*At thirty-one minutes, she requested it again.*

*At thirty-eight minutes, she ceased pretending the requests were intended as instructions.*

*The bracers performed satisfactorily.*

Below the signature, Kashya had added a line in smaller writing.

*I should not have found the distinction as satisfying as I did.*

Mira became aware that she was still smiling, though the reason had changed.

She searched the crate.

The black volume beneath Charsi’s diary had no title, only the letter **K** pressed into its cover. It contained dates spanning eighteen years. Every entry began with the formality of a patrol report. Almost every entry concerned Cordelia, the cleaned eastern jail chamber and an expanding collection of equipment built by Charsi.

Mira looked toward the archive door.

Nothing in her assignment prohibited reading uncatalogued field records. Nothing in her training suggested she should do so with her legs drawn together and both books open upon her lap.

She began cross-referencing dates.


### The Poise Frame

Charsi’s drawing showed a tall timber frame with an overhead counterweight, wrist attachment and a floor platform raised by a screw mechanism.

*Cordelia wants the wearer held upright with her hands behind her while the floor lowers beneath her feet. At the first setting she can stand normally. At the second she must rise onto the fronts of her feet. At the third only her toes can touch.*

*I asked why the floor needs a fourth setting.*

*Cordelia said, “For honesty.”*

*I have reinforced the overhead beam.*

Charsi added individual toe rests which could be withdrawn separately. Her notes expressed particular concern about cramps, circulation and the strain placed upon high arches. She lined the wrist suspension with the same silk used inside the bracers and installed a safety catch capable of supporting twice Cordelia’s weight.

Kashya’s entry was dated the following evening.

*Cordelia entered the frame barefoot and summoned the anklets after her wrists were secured. Her clothing transformed as expected. The stockings remained; the shoes appeared only when the platform began descending.*

*At setting two, the heels provided no meaningful support. At setting three, she balanced upon the tips of her toes for nine minutes.*

*I removed the left toe rest.*

*Cordelia asked me to replace it. The agreed word was not used.*

*I removed the right rest.*

Mira’s gaze lingered on the next paragraph.

*The bracers carried her weight with both hands bound behind her. Her feet remained pointed several inches above the platform. She attempted to reach it whenever the frame swayed, curling her toes toward a surface that was no longer there.*

*Her requests became increasingly sincere.*

*I did not replace either rest.*

The report ended with treatment for strained calves, warm water for Cordelia’s feet and a declaration that the device would not be used again.

The following entry was entitled **SECOND EVALUATION: POISE FRAME**.

Mira rested her elbow upon the crate and continued.


### The Heel Stocks

*Cordelia has asked for ankle braces compatible with every form of her transformed shoes. They must hold both feet in the highest possible arch, keep the heels separated and leave the toes and soles accessible.*

*She insists that “accessible” is an engineering requirement.*

*Kashya agreed.*

*I am beginning to suspect they discuss these commissions before visiting me.*

Charsi constructed the stocks from two curved steel plates mounted upon a low timber base. Hinges opened beneath the arches. Individual loops could hold each toe extended or permit it to curl. The ankle braces slid outward along a measured rail, allowing Kashya to separate Cordelia’s legs without altering the punishing angle of her feet.

*The device fits beautifully,* Charsi wrote. *Cordelia has narrow heels and very high arches, which required additional shaping. She laughed when I said so and then became embarrassed when I needed to trace both feet.*

*I do not understand why someone proud of her feet objects to having them measured.*

Kashya understood.

*The heel stocks prevented Cordelia from withdrawing her feet or reducing their arch. She remained capable of moving her toes until I attached the individual loops.*

*A soft brush across the left sole caused greater struggling than the crop had produced across both thighs. This was unexpected.*

*Cordelia claims it was not.*

*She has forbidden me from telling Charsi why the toe loops require reinforcement.*

Mira glanced involuntarily at her own boots. Rogue footwear was practical, low-heeled and built for long patrols. She flexed her toes inside them and imagined the measured rail carrying her ankles apart.

She stopped doing that.


### The Disciplinary Cabinet

Charsi did not call this a contraption. She called it an equipment order and spent six pages discussing handles.

Cordelia commissioned a broad suede flogger, a narrow braided whip, a short crop marked at one-inch intervals and several flexible canes. Charsi balanced every handle to Kashya’s grip. She tested the suede against her own forearm, rejected two leather braids for inconsistent weight and added replaceable padded tips to the canes.

*Kashya asked me to mark the safe striking zones upon a wooden figure. Cordelia volunteered to demonstrate the inaccurate zones in person.*

*Kashya told her to put her clothes back on.*

*Cordelia had not removed them yet.*

The clamps required more adjustment.

*Cordelia requested a set small enough for nipples, with screw controls that cannot exceed a safe pressure. She then requested a second set for “other sensitive areas.”*

*I knew what she meant.*

*I wish the record to show that I knew immediately and merely asked for precise measurements because imprecision is dangerous.*

Charsi lined the clamps, calibrated their screws and fitted detachable chains which could connect them to collars, cuffs or counterweights. Kashya’s cabinet inventory listed every piece by weight and maximum pressure.

Her private entry abandoned measurements after the first page.

*Cordelia wore only the bracelets and Andariel’s rings. I attached the nipple clamps below the silver ornaments and joined them by chain to the rearward bracers.*

*Every attempt to lower her bound hands increased the pull across her breasts.*

*I used the suede flogger first. Cordelia requested the crop after twelve strokes. I refused until twenty.*

*She accused me of enjoying the authority.*

*This was an attempt to provoke a reaction.*

An ink blot followed.

*It succeeded.*

Kashya’s next paragraph described checking every mark, loosening the clamps and apologizing for leaving Cordelia’s nipples darker than intended.

*Cordelia reminded me that the pressure never exceeded the agreed limit.*

*I told her agreement did not prevent regret.*

*She asked whether I regretted the session or how much I enjoyed it.*

*I did not answer.*

Mira noticed that her breathing had grown quieter, as though Kashya might somehow hear her across twenty years.


### The Denial Chair

The chair was Charsi’s most elaborate mechanism to that date: a reclining frame with reinforced wrist locks behind the backrest, padded thigh supports and a central assembly operated by gears beneath the seat.

*Cordelia insists that the wearer’s hips must remain completely still. She also wants attachments which stop moving whenever she is close to orgasm.*

*I explained that a machine cannot know this.*

*She returned the next morning with a detection rune.*

*The rune knows.*

Cordelia’s magic sensed muscular tension, pulse and arousal. The mechanical attachment could stimulate her clitoris or enter her vagina according to the configuration selected by Kashya. Whenever the wearer neared climax, the rune disengaged the gears. Once her arousal declined, motion resumed.

Charsi’s final construction note read:

*I tested the stopping mechanism using a pressure gauge. Cordelia said this was inadequate. Kashya said it was preferable.*

*They returned after closing the workshop.*

Kashya’s entry began with an apology to herself.

*This device should not have been built.*

*Cordelia remained secured for seventy-four minutes. The detection rune stopped the mechanism fourteen times. After the ninth interruption she began pleading before it had fully disengaged.*

*I could have overridden the rune at any point.*

*I did not.*

The cold simplicity of those sentences affected Mira more than description would have. She shifted upon the stone floor and discovered warmth gathering beneath her trousers.

Kashya’s handwriting became less even near the bottom of the page.

*At sixty-nine minutes, Cordelia stated that she genuinely needed release.*

*The agreed word remained available.*

*I asked whether “needed” meant “withdrew consent.” She said no.*

*I denied her once more.*

*At seventy-four minutes I permitted the mechanism to continue.*

The next line had been written after the ink dried.

*I have never heard my own name spoken in that manner.*


### The Transport Cage

Charsi objected to the dimensions.

*Cordelia requested a cage too low for standing and too narrow for turning around comfortably. I offered to enlarge it. She refused.*

*The interior contains a ridged saddle mounted along the center rail. A collar leash passes through the forward bars and controls whether the wearer can reach it.*

*This is not transportation equipment.*

The declaration represented a notable development in Charsi’s understanding.

She nevertheless rounded the bars, padded the floor where bare knees would rest and installed an emergency hinge beneath the saddle. Kashya’s report described making Cordelia crawl from one end of the cage to the other with her wrists cuffed behind her. The high heels repeatedly caught between the bars. Whenever Cordelia managed to settle upon the saddle, Kashya shortened the leash and removed the contact she had earned.

*Cordelia called this unfair.*

*I reminded her that fairness had not appeared in the commission.*

*She begged me to loosen the leash.*

*I required her to ask properly.*

Mira turned the page.

The remainder of the entry had been cut out with a knife.

Charsi’s diary supplied an explanation three days later.

*Kashya visited the forge and burned two pages from her own journal in my quenching basin.*

*She said they contained tactical errors.*

*Cordelia said the tactics had been excellent.*

*Kashya left.*


### The Runic Chastity Belt

The belt consisted of polished steel, dark leather and a narrow series of silver channels. Its fitted plates prevented the wearer from touching her clitoris or entering her vagina. Five sockets along the front accepted interchangeable runes designed by Cordelia.

Charsi catalogued them carefully.

*Ember maintains arousal.*

*Denial prevents orgasm.*

*Command allows only the attuned key-holder to grant release.*

*Exposure alters clothing around the belt without opening it.*

*Mercy releases every lock if the wearer genuinely withdraws consent.*

*Cordelia says the Mercy rune must always be installed. Kashya agrees. I have therefore made its socket impossible to remove without destroying the belt.*

The practical care beneath the indecent design quieted something in Mira. Whatever occurred in the eastern chamber, the women involved had built trust into steel.

Kashya’s first entry concerning the belt was almost entirely procedural. The second was not.

*Cordelia wore Ember and Denial beneath ordinary robes for the duration of evening patrol reports. She remained capable of conversation for twenty-three minutes.*

*At thirty minutes she stood behind my chair because sitting had become uncomfortable.*

*At forty-one she asked to be excused.*

*I reminded her that Command required my permission.*

*She remained.*

*None of the other officers noticed.*

Below this, in darker ink:

*Charsi noticed immediately when Cordelia entered the forge afterward.*

*Charsi notices more than she admits.*


### The Discipline Wheel

The wheel occupied an entire foldout page.

It was tall enough to hold Cordelia with arms and legs extended, padded along the spine and fitted with rails accepting every cuff, clamp and ankle brace Charsi had previously built. A geared axle allowed one person to rotate it. Locking pins stopped the wearer upright, horizontal, inverted or at any angle between.

*Cordelia calls it modular,* Charsi wrote. *Kashya calls it excessive. I call it difficult to fit through the jail doors.*

They had removed the doorframe.

Kashya’s report listed six positions, four implements and one occasion upon which Cordelia’s summoned heels became entangled in a chain. The error caused no injury. Kashya nevertheless devoted two paragraphs to criticizing herself and three to the way Cordelia had comforted her afterward.

The final sentence read:

*I regret every session for approximately one day. Then Cordelia returns, and I regret how little the regret matters.*

Mira closed the black diary.

She had been in the archive for several hours. Supper had probably begun. Her original inventory assignment lay unfinished beside her, suddenly less compelling than the eastern chamber appearing throughout both journals.

An old storage manifest inside the crate listed the jail equipment as **RETAINED FOR HISTORICAL AND TRAINING PURPOSES**.

Someone had written **EASTERN CHAMBER** beneath it.

A small iron key had been tied to the page with faded blue cord.

The label attached to it bore Charsi’s handwriting.

*Kashya said this should be destroyed. She did not specify when.*

Mira knew where the eastern chamber was.

That knowledge did not require her to stand, tuck both diaries beneath her arm and take the key.

She did so anyway.


### The Unified Spreader

The old jail no longer smelled of blood.

Twenty years of soap, limewash and open braziers had transformed the Monastery’s lower levels into storage, training rooms and memorial chambers. The dead had been removed. The demons had not returned. Fresh Rogue sigils marked the passages, and ordinary lamps replaced the hellish light Mira knew from campaign paintings.

The eastern chamber waited behind a reinforced oak door.

Charsi’s key opened it.

Inside, clean linen covered large, angular shapes. Mira closed the door, lit two wall lamps and drew back the nearest sheet.

The Discipline Wheel stood beneath it, leather conditioned and metal free of rust.

Nothing in the chamber had been abandoned. The whips hung by length inside their cabinet. The clamps had been cleaned and arranged upon velvet. The Poise Frame’s screw turned beneath Mira’s cautious hand without resistance. A fresh container of leather oil sat beside the Transport Cage.

Someone still maintained the room.

Mira should have left then.

Instead she uncovered the final object.

It was much simpler than the others: one polished steel bar, a little longer than Mira was tall, with four padded cuffs mounted along its length. Two ankle cuffs occupied the widely separated ends. Two smaller wrist cuffs could slide between them and lock at any chosen distance.

Charsi’s plate read **UNIFIED SPREADER — COMMISSION C.V.**

The corresponding diary pages had been tucked beneath the frame.

*Cordelia requested ankle cuffs at both ends and two wrist cuffs between them. I explained that this would hold her hands between her spread legs and make standing effectively impossible.*

*She said that was exactly correct.*

*I asked whether she had solved her own design problem before coming to me.*

*Apparently she wanted the bar polished.*

Kashya’s report was brief.

*With Cordelia seated, the bar held her ankles apart and both hands low between her thighs. Reclining drew her knees outward and left no useful means of covering herself. The device required no additional restraint.*

*Cordelia called its simplicity elegant.*

*I called it effective.*

Mira ran one thumb over the nearest cuff. The padding remained soft. Each lock used a spring catch which closed when pressure entered it. A release rail ran along the underside of the bar, accessible to someone standing before the wearer but not, according to Charsi’s diagram, to the wearer herself.

It was an important mechanical distinction.

Mira decided to verify it.

She removed her boots first. The archive dust upon them had no place on preserved equipment. Then she unbuckled her outer leather armor because its rigid skirt interfered with sitting behind the bar. She retained her light training tunic, fitted trousers and underlayers. Nothing about this was indecent. Rogues trained in less.

Nothing about it was necessary either.

Mira sat on a folded mat with the bar before her. She placed her left ankle into the end cuff and pressed until the spring catch closed. The right followed. Steel now kept her legs in a broad V, not painful but far wider than ordinary rest.

She tested the underside release rail. Both ankle cuffs opened immediately.

Functional.

Mira closed them again.

The wrist cuffs slid smoothly. She adjusted them near the center, then placed her left hand into one. The catch folded over her wrist with a firm metallic click. Her arm now extended downward between her open legs.

The second wrist required Mira to lean forward. She hesitated with her hand above it.

Charsi’s drawing showed that all four locks could be released by the rail.

Mira pressed her wrist down.

Click.

For a moment nothing changed.

Then she attempted to reach the release rail.

Both hands were fixed palm-down near the center of the bar. Her fingers could curl around its upper edge but could not pass beneath it. Moving one wrist drew the other no closer to freedom. The ankle cuffs kept the ends beyond reach. When Mira tried to stand, the bar rose with her legs and pulled her weight forward until she dropped back onto the mat.

“Excellent,” she whispered.

The word sounded less convincing in the empty chamber.

She tried reclining as Kashya’s report described. The bar came toward her hips. Her knees bent and opened farther, while both wrists remained trapped low between her thighs. The position pulled her tunic upward along her waist and stretched the fitted trousers intimately across the center of her body.

Mira sat up again.

That movement was possible. Dignified movement was not.

She searched the floor for the key. It lay beside her discarded armor, two feet beyond the left end of the bar.

Mira extended one bare foot toward it. The ankle cuff allowed her toes to move but kept the heel fixed. She reached, curled her toes and pushed the key farther away.

“Excellent,” she repeated.

This time it was clearly a criticism.

The chamber door opened.

Mira became very still.

Kashya entered first.

Twenty years had silvered more of the commander’s dark hair and drawn fine lines beside her eyes. Age had not diminished the economy of her movement or the sensation that every room became a formation when she stepped into it. She wore a dark training coat over close leather and carried a familiar iron key case at her belt.

Her gaze found Mira, the missing archive key, the removed armor and the Unified Spreader.

It became glacial.

Cordelia entered behind her.

The legendary Sorceress was forty-seven and looked no older than her late thirties, a benefit of Zann Esu magic she wore as naturally as good health. Her long black hair carried a narrow silver streak at each temple and fell in a heavy braid over one shoulder. Travel had preserved the graceful strength in her legs and waist; time had deepened rather than softened the confidence of her amber-brown eyes. She carried the Horadric Cube beneath one arm and a pack full enough to suggest another extended Grail run.

She stopped beside Kashya.

Mira sat against the wall with her legs spread by the polished bar and both hands locked helplessly between them.

Cordelia examined the device.

“Charsi polished it beautifully.”

Kashya turned her head. “That is your first observation?”

“It was my first design requirement.”

Mira attempted to draw her knees together. The steel bar treated the effort as theoretical.

“Captain,” she began.

Kashya’s attention returned to her. “You are Archivist Mira Thorne.”

The fact that Kashya knew her name made everything worse.

“Yes.”

“You removed restricted journals from the east archive.”

“They were stored in an unrestricted equipment crate.”

Cordelia looked at Kashya. “That sounds like a filing error.”

“Charsi,” they said together.

Mira tried to recover professional ground. “I was verifying whether the preserved mechanisms remained operational.”

Cordelia glanced at the bar. “They do.”

“I had reached that conclusion.”

Kashya crossed the room and crouched before her. The older woman did not touch the release. She first examined the ankle cuffs, checked the color of Mira’s feet and pressed two fingers beneath each wrist restraint to confirm circulation.

Only then did she ask, “Are you hurt?”

“No.”

“Numbness?”

“No.”

“Panic?”

Mira considered the question honestly. Her heart was beating too quickly. Heat had spread across her face and farther down, made worse by Cordelia’s curious attention and Kashya’s hands checking the restraints. None of it was panic.

“No.”

Kashya sat back upon her heels.

“Do you want to be released?”

The sensible answer waited.

Mira had spent the afternoon reading Kashya deny Cordelia’s requests while carefully protecting every boundary beneath them. She had followed the key into this chamber, removed her own armor and closed all four cuffs. The accidental portion of her predicament had ended the moment Kashya offered release.

Mira looked at the older commander. Then she looked beyond her at Cordelia, whose private desires had commissioned half the chamber and whose expression now held amused recognition rather than judgment.

“Not immediately,” Mira said.

Cordelia’s smile arrived slowly.

Kashya closed her eyes for one long moment.

“I almost regret keeping this room.”

“No, you don’t,” Cordelia said.

Kashya opened her eyes and fixed Mira with the same cold attention preserved throughout the diary.

“Curiosity is not consent.”

“I know.”

“And remaining locked because you are embarrassed to ask for release would not be consent either.”

“I know.”

“Then tell me clearly.”

Mira felt the bar beneath her palms, the cuffs around wrists and ankles, and the impossible openness of the position Charsi had engineered so well.

“I want to remain,” she said.

Silence held for one breath.

Kashya stood.

Cordelia lowered her traveling pack to the floor.

“Well,” the Sorceress said. “That saves time.”

“It does not,” Kashya replied.

She removed the key case from her belt and set it deliberately beyond Mira’s reach.

“It means,” Kashya said, “that we begin properly.”
