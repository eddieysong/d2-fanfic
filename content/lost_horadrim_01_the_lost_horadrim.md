# The Lost Horadrim

Cordelia returned from her thirty-seventh visit to the Forgotten Tower with two chipped topazes, a cracked sash and another copy of Cathan's Seal.

The ring lay in her palm with the smug red gleam of an item which knew perfectly well that she already owned six. Rain silvered the Rogue Encampment beyond the awning of Cain's wagon. Her traveling armor had returned to its practical shape when she dismissed the voluntary jewelry at the edge of camp, though the sensation of its absence still lingered around her wrists and ankles like the memory of hands. Mud darkened her boots. Damp black hair clung in escaped strands to her temples, and a flush remained beneath the warm bronze skin of her throat from exertion, embarrassment and several events she did not intend to enter into the Grail ledger.

Cain examined the ring through a lens.

"Cathan's Seal," he said.

"I had reached a similar conclusion from the inscription reading *Cathan's Seal*."

"Identification is not improved by resentment."

"Neither is the ring."

He placed it beside five identical bands on the corner of his worktable. The arrangement had begun as temporary storage and was acquiring the character of an accusation.

"You may be more interested in what I discovered while you were gone."

That sentence changed the air more effectively than any spell. Cordelia lowered herself onto the stool opposite him, fatigue receding behind immediate attention. Cain had spent the weeks since the Worldstone studying every surviving record of the Cairn Stones, the blessing and the Beneficent jewelry. Most of his discoveries had been variations upon *ancient*, *inadvisable* and *insufficient information*. He did not generally summon interest unless he had something capable of feeding it.

Upon the table rested a narrow folio bound in grey leather. Its edges were blackened, and several pages had been repaired with strips of parchment whose handwriting ran contrary to the original text. A Horadric seal occupied the cover. Someone had scored a line through it so fiercely that the blade had cut into the leather beneath.

"I believe I have identified the creator of your blessing," Cain said.

Cordelia looked first at the folio, then at him. "Was he a demon?"

"No."

"Mad?"

"Not in any sense his contemporaries could establish."

"That answer was unnecessarily careful."

"Edran Vohl was Horadrim."

Rain whispered over the canvas. Somewhere near the forge, Charsi dropped a length of iron and expressed an opinion about it which carried clearly across the camp.

Cordelia leaned nearer the book. "One of yours made the stones offer endangered women elaborate sexual alternatives to death?"

"He is no more one of mine than every Zann Esu who has ever frozen her own boots is one of yours."

"That happened once."

"Vohl's expulsion also happened once. Its consequences have proved rather more durable."

Cain opened the folio. The first pages contained copies of correspondence between names Cordelia recognized from Horadric histories, followed by diagrams of circles, transference matrices and the five Cairn Stones. A repeated sigil appeared in every working: three interlocked loops around a small open center. She had seen that mark before, inscribed so finely upon Andariel's rings that it vanished whenever she looked directly at it.

Seeing it on paper made her nipples tighten in remembered sympathy.

Cordelia folded her arms. Cain, mercifully, was reading.

Edran Vohl had been a gifted spellcaster even by the unreasonable standards of an order founded to imprison Prime Evils. He had mastered Vizjerei binding forms, studied the Zann Esu distinction between compulsion and chosen discipline, and produced enchantments capable of responding not merely to spoken language but to intention. His early work concerned voluntary limitation: magic by which a person could surrender movement, power or authority while preserving a deeper right to revoke that surrender.

"Useful principles," Cain said. "They influenced later Horadric wards, oath seals and training circles. Vohl understood that a restraint which must be monitored from outside is only as trustworthy as its keeper. He attempted to place judgment within the enchantment itself."

Cordelia thought of the silent decision that had remained hers through every accepted ordeal. Monsters had ignored her pleading, the jewelry had sabotaged her magic, and the blessing had delighted in circumstances which left dignity with very little useful work to perform. Yet beneath everything, perfectly distinct from discomfort or desperate need, the power had always known whether she still chose to remain.

"He succeeded," she said.

"Eventually. Your Cairn-Stone blessing appears to have been an early field trial."

"Field trial."

"Vohl's term."

"I crossed five acts wearing the prototype?"

Cain adjusted his spectacles. "Technically, you crossed four and most of a fifth wearing its accumulating reward structure."

"That distinction has made everything better."

Cain continued with the fixed patience of a man who had survived Tristram by refusing to hurry. Vohl's intelligence had never been disputed. His priorities had.

The earliest disciplinary complaint accused him of spending restricted archive funds upon unusually soft rope. A second concerned an experimental ward which had bound three senior Horadrim by their wrists during a lecture on escape magic. A third recorded that Vohl had submitted a one-hundred-and-seventy-page defense entitled *Concerning the Beneficial Applications of Voluntary Helplessness, Compelled Posture and Intimate Authority*.

"Bondage," Cordelia said.

Cain's mouth compressed. "His preferred terminology was more extensive."

"His preferred terminology sounds like bondage wearing ceremonial robes."

"That opinion was expressed at the time."

It had not deterred him. As other Horadrim refined soulstones and hunted demonic corruption, Edran Vohl became fascinated by the magical possibilities of restraint: the sensations produced when strength could not become movement; the heightened awareness of a body whose posture was chosen by another; the psychological difference between being unable to escape and wanting not to possess an easy escape. His private notebooks moved from abstract theory into increasingly practical designs.

Cordelia imagined sober men in hooded robes turning pages that contained diagrams of wrists secured behind backs, ankles held apart and enchantments designed to make pleading an unreliable form of release. She felt an unhelpful warmth low in her abdomen.

"How long did the order tolerate this?"

"Considerably longer than the official histories imply. Vohl's work remained sound. Some of it was brilliant. His peers objected less to the subject than to his insistence upon presenting it at every available opportunity."

"A familiar scholarly weakness."

Cain gave her a look over his spectacles.

"I am listening respectfully."

"You are imagining the diagrams."

Cordelia allowed one eyebrow to rise. "Those activities are not mutually exclusive."

Vohl's final presentation to the Horadric council had involved a device described only as an adjustable restraint chair and a request for volunteers. No volunteers had been forthcoming. Vohl had apparently regarded this as a failure of academic courage and begun demonstrating its features on himself.

The surviving account ended there.

"They expelled him for tying himself to a chair?" Cordelia asked.

"They expelled him for refusing to stop explaining why the chair was important after the council adjourned. It took two days to locate the release command."

Despite herself, Cordelia liked him a little.

That was unfortunate, because Vohl had been dead for centuries. After his expulsion, he disappeared into the eastern kingdoms and established several laboratories under false names. The Horadrim erased him from their rolls, confiscated the work they could find and sealed whatever resisted examination. In time, wars scattered the order and its archives. Vohl survived only in crossed-out citations, embarrassed letters and the occasional enchantment no later scholar could reproduce.

The Cairn-Stone blessing had endured because it had been embedded in a waystone network older than the Rogue Monastery. Its voice, its lecherous enthusiasm and the jewelry keyed to the Lesser and Prime Evils all bore Vohl's signature. Cain believed it had been intended to prove that a powerful subject could remain meaningfully free while accepting conditions which removed every outward sign of freedom.

"He chose monsters because they were beyond negotiation," Cain said. "The wearer could not rely upon their mercy, understanding or obedience. Only upon the enchantment recognizing her own decision."

Cordelia remembered Rakanishu's knife suspended beneath her ribs while a cheerful voice explained exactly what the Fallen would do instead of killing her. She remembered the Countess, Duriel, Sszark's living silk, Diablo's hands and the complete set holding her beneath Baal until pleasure became indistinguishable from catastrophe.

She also remembered accepting.

"That part worked," she said quietly.

Cain's expression softened, though his scholarship did not. "Yes. It did."

He turned several pages. The three-loop sigil changed subtly in the later diagrams, its open center becoming a small diamond.

"The blessing and jewelry were not Vohl's final work," he said. "They may have been among his first complete creations."

Cordelia became very still.

Cain noticed. "That was not an invitation."

"I have not moved."

"You have acquired the expression which normally precedes movement."

The recovered folio was not Vohl's catalogue. It was an inventory assembled by those who pursued him after his expulsion. Most entries were fragments: an artifact name, a workshop location, a warning copied without context. Four were detailed enough to establish that the objects had survived their creator.

Cain placed a finger beneath the first.

"The Cord of Willing Constraint. Later marginalia calls it the Penitent Coil."

The illustration showed an immaculate length of pale rope looped upon itself. The accompanying notes claimed that it recognized its owner, understood broad spoken commands and constructed knots without human assistance. It could bind the owner or be cast upon another willing, attuned subject. Unlike an ordinary enchanted rope, it did not merely immobilize. It selected positions, adjusted pressure and used movement against the person it held.

"Against?" Cordelia asked.

Cain read another line and developed a sudden interest in translating it twice.

"Struggling may cause the Coil to tighten or to move across... responsive anatomical regions."

"Sensitive places."

"That is the less formal rendering."

The rope could draw a harness across breasts, place a sliding knot between the thighs, wind around arches and toes, or suspend its wearer so that every attempt at leverage produced some carefully calculated friction. It obeyed instructions to ignore later pleas or requests for early release, provided those instructions had been knowingly given before the binding. Yet the enchantment was said to recognize genuine distress and free its subject without waiting for a command.

No single knot interested Cordelia as much as that intelligence. The Coil understood the useful fiction she had learned to enjoy: *Do not let me out simply because I make a convincing argument once I am desperate.* It could preserve that fiction without ever confusing it for the deeper decision beneath.

"Where is it?"

"Unknown. One report places it beneath the Halls of the Dead. Another says Vohl carried it east."

"So there are two leads."

"There are two contradictory statements."

"That is what leads generally are before someone follows them."

Cain moved his finger down the page with more force than necessary.

The second artifact was not an object but a rune called the Rose Sentence. Invocation fused it into the wearer's skin as a pink tattoo low upon the abdomen. The wearer selected a term in advance. Her body could still be driven through the physical completion of orgasm, but the pleasure itself would be muted at the decisive instant and drawn into the inscription. The bodily pressure would reset; satisfaction would not. Desire and sensitivity increased rather than diminished, ensuring that she recovered with humiliating speed and that every subsequent theft became easier to provoke. The surviving description stated that the stolen pleasure was *conserved*, but the final clause explaining its disposition had been lost with the lower corner of the page.

Cordelia read the paragraph herself.

"There is an early-release provision," she observed.

"A deliberately expensive one. The rune distinguished true withdrawal from frustration. Genuine distress ended the working safely. A wearer who still wanted the sentence but wished to purchase satisfaction from her own impatience had to sacrifice a rune of value proportionate to the remaining term."

A table survived. One day required a Lem. A week required a Gul. One month required a Cham.

Cordelia calculated automatically.

Her stash contained two Sur runes and three Ber runes. The Sur pair could become another Ber. Four Ber could become two Jah, and two Jah could become one Cham. She could therefore escape a month-long Rose Sentence early.

She could also use two of her current Ber runes to make a Jah, pair it with the third Ber and finally assemble Enigma, which had been the purpose of hoarding them through months of insultingly fruitless runs.

Cain watched the arithmetic cross her face. "You are not presently under the effect."

"I know."

"Then why do you look protective of your rune stash?"

"Because a Cham is an irresponsible price for one orgasm."

"An encouraging conclusion."

"Especially when the same resources nearly make an Enigma."

His encouragement died with admirable dignity.

The third entry concerned the Mutable Instrument. Its recovered sketch depicted a smooth silver object with neither fixed dimensions nor identifiable purpose. Vohl described it as an adaptive intimate implement capable of reading a subject's body and assuming the form best suited to the command it had received. Reports mentioned a phallus, a plug, both together, a wearable harness and several configurations the pursuing Horadrim had declined to draw.

Like the Coil, it possessed judgment. Its owner could use it upon herself or an attuned companion, set broad boundaries and then allow it to change shape, depth, pressure and rhythm according to response. Later notes suggested that Vohl had designed the two artifacts to recognize one another.

Cordelia looked from the drawing of the silver Instrument to the pale Coil above it.

"They work together."

"That interpretation is possible."

"The rope chooses a position and the Instrument chooses what the position is for."

Cain closed his eyes briefly. "That interpretation is regrettably persuasive."

Cordelia could imagine the Coil securing wrists, arranging legs and deciding exactly how much movement remained possible while the Instrument adapted to every involuntary reaction. The image was only theoretical. It nevertheless entered her body with enough clarity that she shifted on the stool.

Cain turned the page before theory could become discussion.

The fourth entry had been copied in a different hand. The letters were pressed so heavily into the parchment that the strokes remained visible upon the sheet beneath.

**THE WHORE-MAKER.**

Nothing illustrated it. No complete description survived. There were only scattered phrases drawn from a sealed laboratory report:

*postural instruction; obedience; pleasure through service; conditioned appetite; temporary adjustment of disposition; voluntary retention—*

Then a line in red ink:

**DO NOT ACTIVATE WITHOUT A COMPLETE TRANSLATION.**

Cordelia read the title again.

"Possibly metaphorical," Cain said.

"No."

"We cannot know that."

"A man expelled for presenting bondage furniture to the Horadric council did not name an artifact the Whore-Maker as a metaphor."

Cain's silence conceded more than his face intended.

The inventory placed Vohl's primary laboratory somewhere below old Kurast, though the directions relied upon landmarks which had disappeared before Travincal fell. Other fragments suggested he had stored artifacts in desert tombs, abandoned sanctums and chambers reached only through waypoints keyed to his personal sigil. None of the locations was certain. All of them were dangerous, unstable or occupied by creatures which had continued living there without appreciating their archival significance.

Cain closed the folio.

"The order which expelled Vohl believed these inventions should remain lost."

"The order which expelled him also lost track of them."

"That is not a recommendation."

"It is not an impressive custodial record."

Cain removed his spectacles and polished them. It was an old maneuver: the scholarly equivalent of placing both hands behind his back before saying something he expected Cordelia to resist.

"Sanctuary is safe," he said. "Baal is defeated. The Worldstone is gone, and your curse with it. These artifacts are not pieces of another obligation. Their creator is long dead. Nobody requires you to recover them, test them or prove anything about yourself through them."

The words landed exactly where they were meant to. Cordelia felt no prophecy closing around her, no imprisoned angel waiting beyond an impossible fight, no camp whose survival depended upon her willingness to continue. If she left the grey folio upon Cain's table, the Coil would remain wherever it had waited for centuries. The Rose Sentence would mark nobody. The silver Instrument would keep its unknown shape. The Whore-Maker could brood beneath Kurast until the jungle finally swallowed its name.

Nobody would blame her.

Nobody would be harmed.

There was no practical reason to go.

Curiosity moved through Cordelia with a slow warmth quite unlike duty. She wanted to see how much more sophisticated Vohl's work had become after the blessing. She wanted to speak a command to a coil of rope and discover what an intelligence devoted to binding would make of her body. She wanted to know whether the Rose Sentence truly could make one month feel impossible—and whether, with an Enigma waiting on the other side of her restraint, she could refuse to pay the price of impatience. She wanted to learn what shape the Mutable Instrument would choose when given permission not to ask her preference every moment.

As for the final device, any artifact with a warning written that deeply into the page had made an unanswerable mistake: it had become interesting.

"I understand," Cordelia said.

Cain put his spectacles back on. "Do you?"

"Completely. This is not my responsibility."

"Correct."

"There is no deadline."

"None."

"No prophecy."

"Certainly not."

"And if I decide after sober investigation that an artifact warrants controlled testing, I would be acting solely from personal choice."

Cain studied her face. "You have omitted several intermediate conclusions."

"They seemed unlikely to survive contact with events."

He placed one hand over the folio before she could reach for it.

"The documents remain here until I have copied the relevant safeguards and translated the surviving location ciphers."

"How long?"

"Several days."

"I could assist."

"Your Old Horadric is inadequate."

"My Old Horadric rescued you from a cage."

"Your fire rescued me from a cage. Your Old Horadric activated the wrong stones because you attempted to sit down."

Cordelia considered this slanderous only in its emphasis.

She left the folio beneath his hand and returned to her tent. The decision not to steal from Deckard Cain occupied no effort at all and deserved, she felt, some recognition.

Her preparations were correspondingly restrained.

She emptied mud from her boots, repaired the cracked sash and moved the Horadric Cube to the top of her traveling pack. Town Portal scrolls went into one side pocket. Potions went into the other. She placed a clean artifact case between two folded robes, followed by chalk, measuring cord, three blank journals and a small hand mirror whose research purpose she preferred not to define in advance.

The rune chest required more thought.

Cordelia opened it and counted twice. Two Sur. Three Ber. Enough, with the correct cubing, to make a Cham. Also enough to complete the important portion of an Enigma and preserve months of effort from becoming the most expensive surrender to impatience in recorded Horadric history.

She locked the chest.

Then she put the key in the inner pocket of her pack.

Outside, Cain's lamp remained bright beneath the wagon awning. Through the wet canvas Cordelia heard the dry turn of ancient pages and his occasional disapproving murmur as Edran Vohl continued offending the Horadric order centuries after death.

The Holy Grail had taught her patience. Thirty-seven runs of the same tower could produce nothing but chipped gems and Cathan's Seal; the thirty-eighth might hold something wonderful. Vohl's artifacts had waited far longer than Cordelia had searched. They could wait several days more while Cain translated their locations and she made the responsible, voluntary, entirely unnecessary decision to find them.

She lay down.

In the dark, one escaped strand of black hair brushed her throat. For an instant it felt like rope.

Cordelia smiled into her pillow.

Sanctuary was saved.

At last, she had time for trouble which did not need solving.
