# Volume II — Forty-Five Days

The ivory volume opened to a calendar.

Seris had expected an index. Emily Vohl had apparently considered dates more honest.

Forty-five squares occupied the first two pages, each bordered in pale rose and crowded with notations in three different hands. The black script was Emily's: disciplined, compact, and infuriatingly neat. Cordelia had written in violet wherever she believed the record required correction. Zephira's later additions were heavier, less patient, and generally appeared beside passages Cordelia would rather have left alone.

Above the calendar stood the title.

**OBSERVATIONS UPON A VOLUNTARY PROLONGED ROSE SENTENCE, UNDERTAKEN FOR THE DEFEAT OF THREE REGENERATIVE PRIME EVILS**

**Duration: forty-five days.**

**Primary subject: Cordelia of the Zann Esu, Sorceress.**

**Principal observer: Emily Vohl, temporarily deceased.**

*C.: You cannot be temporarily deceased for several centuries.*

*E.: I can manifest a pulse. The distinction is academic.*

*Z.: That is the first honest use of the word academic in these books.*

Seris pressed her lips together. The archives were silent around her. It was late enough that the lamps in the outer stacks had been extinguished, leaving her island of candlelight alone among the shelves.

She turned the page.

## Terms and Mechanism

Emily began with the point most likely to be misunderstood.

The Rose Sentence did not prevent Cordelia's body from reaching orgasm. It did not require a partner to tease her until the last possible instant and withdraw. Such timing, Emily observed, was unreliable, exhausting, and dramatically overrated.

Instead the Rose permitted the entire physical sequence. Cordelia could be driven through the trembling ascent, the involuntary contractions, the breathless loss of coordination and the bodily reset afterward. At the decisive instant, however, the pleasure itself was muted and drawn away. The rose inscription absorbed it, stored it, and left its wearer with the physical evidence of release but without satisfaction.

The distinction mattered.

Withdrawal at the edge left pressure suspended. The Rose removed the pressure and preserved the hunger. Cordelia's body softened, recovered, and became ready again; her mind received none of the completion it had been promised. Worse, the enchantment maintained an elevated baseline of arousal and increased sensitivity as the stored reserve grew. Each stolen climax therefore made the next easier to reach, harder to resist, and no more satisfying than the last.

**The denial is not an interruption of the process,** Emily wrote. **It is a successful process with its meaning removed.**

Seris read that sentence three times.

In the margin, Cordelia had written:

*C.: This is technically exact and emotionally vicious.*

*E.: A useful summary of my best work.*

The original sentence had been forty days. The wearer could end it for genuine distress without penalty; the enchantment was not permitted to confuse panic, injury, or withdrawal of consent with ordinary pleading. Impatience was different. An early release requested merely because Cordelia had become desperate would cost a rune of sufficient value to make desperation compete with economics.

Cordelia had owned the necessary runes.

She had also wanted an Enigma.

*C.: This paragraph makes me sound miserly.*

*Z.: You endured six weeks of magical denial rather than spend a rune.*

*C.: Enigma is very good.*

*Z.: Miserly and correct are not mutually exclusive.*

Seris set the book down long enough to pour herself water. The cup clicked once against her teeth.

## Days One Through Thirteen: Adaptation

The first entries were almost disappointingly orderly.

Day One established baseline pulse, sensitivity, recovery time and spell stability. Cordelia was cheerful. She made three separate jokes about the sentence being less frightening once one understood the mechanism. Emily recorded all three and appended the time at which Cordelia stopped making them.

The first stolen climax surprised her despite the explanation. Her body completed itself with helpless conviction: muscles tightening, back lifting, breath breaking into a cry she had not intended to make. Then the center of the sensation vanished. What should have spread through her in warm, shuddering waves became a pale echo, enough to tell her precisely what had been taken.

She lay still afterward, blinking at the ceiling.

“That,” she reportedly said, “is astonishingly unfair.”

**Subject understood the mechanism before initiation,** Emily noted. **Understanding did not reduce its effect. It may have increased it by permitting anticipation.**

By Day Four Cordelia's recovery time had shortened measurably. By Day Seven she could be brought back to full receptivity before the woman helping her had finished laughing at her previous expression. Her pride adapted less quickly than her body. She continued attempting casual conversation immediately afterward, as though a steady voice might disguise the way her thighs pressed together or the tremor in her hands.

It did not.

Charsi responded with worried sweetness and proved incapable of concealing when she was fascinated. Atma preferred patient hands, warm oil and a tavern keeper's instinct for knowing exactly when a guest was pretending not to need something. Anya treated every session as a problem of magical precision. Kashya brought discipline, economy, and an unnerving ability to wait through Cordelia's composure until one quiet observation made it collapse.

Emily did not flatten them into interchangeable operators. She recorded the difference each woman made.

**Subject responds strongly to kindness when kindness offers no escape. Subject responds still more strongly to competence.**

*C.: “Subject” had a name.*

*E.: The subject objected whenever the name was accompanied by an accurate adjective.*

*Z.: Which adjective?*

*E.: Receptive.*

*C.: I regret giving both of you ink.*

Seris shifted on the plain archive stool. The enchanted chair remained two feet away, politely unused. She could not help wondering whether Volume II was measuring her regardless, then found a note tucked into the gutter as if Emily had anticipated the suspicion.

**No reader observations are retained without express permission. I am shameless, not discourteous.**

Below it, in Zephira's hand:

*A distinction she makes whenever convenient.*

## Days Fourteen Through Twenty-Seven: Receptivity

The second phase abandoned the fiction that Cordelia could fit the sentence neatly around an ordinary life.

She still trained. She still sorted loot, argued over prices and planned the Uber expedition. Yet arousal had become the quiet condition beneath everything else. Cloth sliding across sensitized skin could steal her attention in the middle of a sentence. A hand resting innocently at her waist made her breath catch. The sound of a lock closing somewhere in the room could draw a visible shiver from her before anyone had touched her at all.

Emily classified this not as loss of judgment but as divided attention under a persistent magical stimulus. Cordelia remained Cordelia: witty, practical, capable of refusing, capable of stopping. She was simply required to exercise those faculties while her body made increasingly eloquent arguments against patience.

On Day Seventeen, Atma asked whether Cordelia wanted the evening left undisturbed.

Cordelia said yes.

Then she stood in the doorway for twelve seconds, came back inside and asked Atma not to be kind about it.

The ensuing entry contained no intimate choreography. Emily recorded only changes in breathing, the speed of Cordelia's recovery, and the language she used before and after the Rose stole the promised relief. Early requests had been witty. Later ones became plain. She stopped asking because the Cataclysm required power and began asking because she needed the pleasure itself.

The Rose took it anyway.

**A voluntary sentence becomes most psychologically effective when the wearer ceases to perform endurance for an external objective,** Emily wrote, **and encounters instead the immediate, humiliating simplicity of wanting.**

Seris's thumb remained against that line until the page warmed beneath it.

Day Twenty-Two contained a brief logistical crisis. Cordelia visited Charsi with her rune stash, set it on the workbench and asked the smith to keep it somewhere inaccessible.

“Because you fear theft?” Charsi asked.

“Because I know exactly how many combinations make a Cham.”

Charsi stared at her. Cordelia stared back with all the dignity available to a woman whose Rose was visibly glowing through the thin cloth over her lower abdomen.

Charsi took the runes.

*C.: Friendship is recognizing when a person needs help protecting herself from terrible financial decisions.*

*Z.: You were willing to fight three Prime Evils but not compound your own runes.*

*C.: Again: Enigma is very good.*

By Day Twenty-Seven, Cordelia no longer trusted sleep to blunt the hunger. She asked Kashya to secure her in the cleaned jail for three days and refuse every ordinary request for early release. The cage was austere, visible, and too small for graceful pacing. Kashya confirmed the terms twice, locked it, and placed the key beyond reach.

Emily's measurements became less bloodless there. Cordelia's arousal had acquired what the observer called a *radiant persistence*: flushed skin, swollen sensitivity, quickened breath, the subtle restless movements of a woman whose body interpreted every surface as a possible source of relief. Kashya, despite formidable composure, was not immune to the sight.

**Observer K displayed increased pulse and prolonged visual attention.**

*K.: Remove this.*

*E.: Declined.*

*C.: Kashya was allowed to annotate this?*

*E.: For thirteen minutes.*

*Z.: I admire her economy.*

Seris looked over her shoulder at the dark archive. No one was there. Her relief was genuine and, annoyingly, not complete.

## Days Twenty-Eight Through Thirty-Nine: Accumulation

The reserve stopped behaving like a collection of discrete stolen climaxes. It became a weather system.

Emily charted the magical pressure within the inscription, the changing color of its petals and the increasingly sympathetic behavior of the Beneficent jewelry. The set stimulated Cordelia more insistently because arousal supplied Arcane energy; the Rose preserved the denied hunger; each mechanism fed the other. Cordelia could spend some of that energy through lesser spells, but she could not empty the stored reserve. The Cataclysm remained sealed behind her deliberate release.

The women helping her learned not to stop when her body began to crest. There was no need. They could follow the response through its full physical conclusion, hold her while the pleasure disappeared, and continue after the brief reset when the Rose restored its hungry baseline.

The unfairness became part of what excited her.

She knew the enchantment would steal the climax. She knew pleading could not change its term. She knew each complete failure would make her warmer, softer, more shamelessly responsive the next time. That knowledge did not discourage her body. It taught it to surrender faster.

On Day Thirty-Six she laughed during a session, then cried, then laughed again because the tears embarrassed her. Anya stopped to ask the one question that mattered. Cordelia answered clearly that she wanted to continue.

Afterward Emily recorded the exchange and nothing else.

**Helplessness without the continuing ability to choose is merely captivity. Helplessness repeatedly chosen, while its consequences are fully understood, is the architecture of the subject's pleasure.**

There were no jokes beneath that sentence.

Seris felt something in her chest loosen. Cain's monograph had made the magic intelligible. Emily's journal made the women intelligible: not victims of an enchantment, not fearless caricatures, but adults choosing a difficult delight and preserving the choice even at the moment it became most inconvenient.

Then she reached Day Thirty-Nine.

The calculated reserve was probably sufficient to overcome the Prime Evils' regeneration. Probably was not a word Emily trusted around Mephisto.

She recommended five additional days.

Cordelia had prepared herself for release on the fortieth. The date had become a shore visible across dark water. When Emily proposed moving it, Cordelia went utterly still.

The journal recorded a private conversation, summarized with Cordelia's later permission. Emily explained the uncertainty. Cordelia called her evil. Emily agreed. Cordelia asked how much safer five days would make the expedition. Emily gave the estimate. Cordelia swore for nearly a minute, laughed once without humor, and authorized the extension.

*C.: I would like the record to show that forty-five is a beautifully round number in certain obscure Sorceress counting systems.*

*Z.: Which systems?*

*C.: The ones invented while desperate.*

## Days Forty Through Forty-Five: The Margin of Safety

The final five squares on the opening calendar had been colored dark red.

Emily described them as the most difficult and the most enthusiastically regretted.

Cordelia had already given the fortieth day the emotional weight of rescue. When it passed without release, anticipation turned back upon her. Every stolen climax carried the additional knowledge that she could have been finished. Every touch arrived in a body trained by forty days to receive it. Every quiet reminder of the extension made humiliation and arousal rise together until she could no longer pretend to distinguish them.

On Day Forty-Two Charsi braced Cordelia against the workbench and discovered that tenderness could be merciless when administered without haste. Atma followed with the patient assurance of someone who knew exactly how desperation changed the meaning of a gentle touch. Cordelia endured several complete bodily releases. The Rose took each one cleanly. Her knees failed after the last, and she laughed into Charsi's shoulder because the alternative was begging again.

She begged anyway.

On Day Forty-Three Anya applied a tincture meant to heighten sensation while dulling injurious pain. The Penitent Coil held Cordelia upright on the points of her toes, offering only enough relief to keep the position possible. Her whole body had become exquisitely legible: nipples reddened by attention, inner thighs trembling, feet curling uselessly for a steadier purchase. Anya and the artifacts brought her through one muted peak after another until her body could reset in moments and her voice had lost every ornamental word.

Emily's note was brief.

**Subject continued by choice. Subject also called the observer a spectral catastrophe. Both statements are true.**

Day Forty-Four belonged to Kashya and the cage. Kashya did not chatter, perform cruelty, or borrow a personality that was not hers. She simply recognized every attempt Cordelia made to recover her composure and waited until it failed. Her restraint made Cordelia's unraveling feel louder.

**Subject's humiliation increased when witnessed by a person she considered exceptionally disciplined. Observer K's arousal increased when subject attempted, unsuccessfully, to conceal her own. Neither finding surprised anyone except Observer K.**

*K.: This remains unnecessary.*

*E.: And remains accurate.*

Day Forty-Five was quieter. They fed Cordelia, bathed her, checked every mark and prepared her equipment. No one tried to exhaust her further. There was no need. The stored reserve had made stillness itself feel like teasing.

At the portal, Cordelia asked Emily whether the Cataclysm would be enough.

Emily said she no longer knew what *enough* meant.

## Release

The account of Uber Tristram occupied less than a page.

Cordelia entered. The three Prime Evils converged. She allowed the encounter to become everything forty-five days had made her crave: dangerous, humiliating, impossible to control by ordinary means. Then she opened the Rose.

All the pleasure it had muted returned together.

The physical force dropped her to her knees before the Arcane force reached its full radius. Every stolen climax—each absent warmth, each denied wave, each frustrated reset—resolved in one colossal release that erased coherent thought. The Cataclysm followed it outward.

Uber Tristram was restored.

The Prime Evils were gone.

Cordelia remained alive, barely articulate and unable to walk properly for several days.

*C.: Two days.*

*E.: You leaned on walls on the third.*

*C.: Walls are architectural companions.*

*Z.: Three days.*

Emily and Deckard Cain came through the portal after the battle. Cain was astonished by the restoration of the town, the destruction of all three Ubers and the intact Hellfire Torch resting near Cordelia's hand.

He identified it proudly.

Three additional levels to Amazon skills.

Cordelia stared at him. Kashya, arriving moments later, accepted the Torch with unusual speed.

The final observation concerned neither magic nor physiology.

**Within one week, subject began calculating the keys required for another run. Observer concludes that prolonged voluntary denial does not cure adventurers of loot.**

*C.: It was a very good run.*

*Z.: The Torch was not even for your class.*

*C.: The next one might be.*

Seris closed the ivory volume carefully.

For several minutes she listened to the small sounds of the archive: cooling stone, candle flame, her own breathing. She had begun the volume believing a forty-five-day experiment would teach her about endurance. Instead it had taught her how desire changed when completion was separated from the body—how wanting could survive success, become more honest under humiliation, and remain chosen even when it ceased to be dignified.

The red third volume waited beside her.

Stamped into its cover was the silhouette of a woman examining herself in a mirror she could not possibly be holding.

Seris reached for it.
