# Volume I — Voluntary Applications

Seris returned Cain's monograph on the seventh day because returning it earlier would have implied alarm and returning it late would have required explaining the fine.

Master Oren examined the silver title, the two blank journals now half full of Seris's precise handwriting and the practical handbook on extinct enchantments whose section on responsive seals had acquired twenty-three paper markers.

"Productive loan?" he asked.

"Historically useful."

"Reproducible?"

Seris had spent six evenings considering that question in increasingly impractical detail.

"Insufficient source material."

Oren entered the return into his ledger. "Then you want Vohl."

Her composure survived because she did not yet know what he meant.

"The historical Vohl?"

"The woman who deposited four volumes and corrected our catalogue entry while I was writing it." Oren opened a lower drawer and found a key with a rose-shaped bit. "Blonde. Walked through the west wall instead of using the door. Called Cain's account admirably responsible and intolerably incomplete."

Seris stared at him.

"You did not mention this last week."

"You asked for books concerning extinct enchantments. Vohl objected to being classified as extinct."

The key opened a cabinet marked **APPLIED BEHAVIORAL ENCHANTMENT: NON-MILITARY**. Four volumes lay inside, bound in black, ivory, red and silver. The black volume bore three interlocked loops surrounding a diamond. Its title changed when Seris lifted it.

**VOLUNTARY APPLICATIONS OF RESPONSIVE ENCHANTMENT**

**Volume I: Recovered Instruments**

**Emily Vohl, formerly of the Horadrim**

A second line wrote itself beneath the author.

**Whose name was never Edran.**

Two notes appeared in the lower margin.

*C.: This correction occupies every title page.*

*E.: It remains correct on every title page.*

A third note arrived in dark-red ink.

*Z.: Edran is a terrible name.*

*E.: Peer review was unanimous.*

Oren placed the key beside Seris. "Seven days. The books dislike food stains, folded corners and dishonest marginalia."

"Books cannot evaluate honesty."

The black cover warmed beneath her fingers.

Oren looked at it, then at her. "That was more or less what I said."

Seris carried all four volumes to the same private reading room in which Cain had clinically dismantled her concentration. She locked the door, arranged fresh paper and opened Volume I.

Emily Vohl's preserved voice was younger than Cain's, lighter and entirely too pleased to have a new reader.

"This catalogue concerns artifacts with no remaining military purpose. The statement is not disparaging. A dining table has little military purpose and still supports civilization more reliably than most councils."

The first page displayed the Penitent Coil, the Rose Sentence, the Mutable Instrument and the Whore-Maker in clean silver linework.

"All four were recovered by Cordelia of the Zann Esu during the first years following Baal's defeat. She calls this period research. Later reviewers have used the words hobby, fixation and expensive way of avoiding ordinary treasure runs."

*C.: I continued treasure runs throughout.*

*Z.: This does not refute fixation.*

The margins allowed touch. When Seris pressed Cordelia's note, earlier revisions unfolded beneath it: Emily calling Cordelia the primary evaluator, Cordelia requesting subject, Emily proposing enthusiastic victim, Cordelia restoring evaluator with three underlines.

Seris smiled despite herself and turned the page.

## The Rose Sentence

Emily's early diagrams showed the Rose as a denial inscription rather than a restraint object. Pink geometry fused into the wearer's lower abdomen for a chosen term. Physical orgasm remained possible; its satisfying pleasure did not. At the final instant, the inscription muted the climax, preserved it and reset bodily pressure without relieving desire.

"The distinction is essential," Emily said. "A subject is not held forever at one edge. She may be stimulated through complete physical orgasm repeatedly. Her contractions occur. Her body descends from the immediate peak. Satisfaction is simply removed before she receives it."

The clinical figure changed. A rose glowed above a sketched woman's sex. Lines representing arousal climbed, broke at orgasm and returned to a baseline higher than before.

"Repeated theft increases sensitivity and shortens recovery. The subject therefore becomes easier to arouse, more visibly responsive and progressively less dignified without suffering uninterrupted physiological strain."

Seris copied the graph.

She copied the words *progressively less dignified* beneath it, then became dissatisfied with how interested her handwriting looked.

Emily briefly described the Rose's recovery from a confiscation inventory compiled by Horadrim who had pursued her after expulsion. Cain found the damaged record; Cordelia followed its eastern workshop coordinates and recovered the intact invocation from beneath a false floor.

"The early-release price was intended to distinguish genuine withdrawal from impatience," Emily explained. "True distress ends the effect safely. A wearer who remains enthusiastically consenting but suddenly dislikes the consequences of her own selected term may purchase release by sacrificing an appropriately painful rune."

*C.: One month required a Cham.*

*E.: Correct.*

*C.: That was extortion.*

*E.: You possessed enough runes.*

*C.: They were reserved for Enigma.*

*Z.: The evaluator valued teleportation more than relief for the entire sentence.*

*C.: This is called discipline.*

*E.: The case notes call it something else.*

Seris touched the final annotation. The page offered a cross-reference to Volume II and displayed a warning:

**THE FORTY-FIVE-DAY FIELD STUDY CONTAINS DIRECT PHYSIOLOGICAL OBSERVATION.**

Her finger remained upon the words long enough for the book to add:

**INTEREST RECORDED. CROSS-REFERENCE SAVED.**

Seris removed her hand.

"Presumptuous," she told it.

The book did not answer aloud. A small marker appeared beside Volume II anyway.

## The Penitent Coil

The Coil occupied more pages than the Rose because Emily had revised it across decades.

Its first design had been ordinary enchanted rope capable of following spoken instructions. The second understood tension, circulation and anatomical limits. The third distinguished a subject's frustrated requests from genuine distress. By the version Cordelia recovered from the sealed workshop, the Coil could compose elaborate restraints, adjust them through movement and recognize emotional evasion with irritating reliability.

"I originally intended it for solitary use," Emily said. "External restraint depends upon the judgment of whoever holds the key. The Coil places judgment inside the enchantment and allows its owner to request an inescapable experience without surrendering safety to an unreliable person."

The illustrated rope formed a chest harness, folded wrists behind the subject and connected ankles to thighs in a supported frog tie. Every line changed color according to pressure.

"It is not surveillance, telepathy or moral authority. It knows the command accepted before the session, reads physical distress and recognizes whether the owner is still choosing the arrangement beneath whatever she says inside it."

*Z.: It also has opinions.*

*E.: Adaptive interpretation.*

*C.: It became a therapist and refused to release me until I described my feelings.*

*E.: You requested a challenging task.*

*C.: I expected knots.*

*Z.: Skill issue.*

*C.: You once ordered four weeks instead of four hours.*

The red note vanished.

Seris touched the empty space. Revision history opened to show that Zephira had attempted to delete Cordelia's reply seven times. Emily had restored it eight.

The academic discussion resumed beneath their argument. Coil tension could stimulate by compression and friction without transforming restraint into injury. Its lines might frame breasts, rub sensitive arches, hold a subject upon bare tiptoes or tighten across the clitoris when she struggled. Yet stimulation remained subordinate to the selected experience. A command for stillness produced stillness. A command for interrogation might preserve denial until honesty satisfied its interpretive model.

Seris studied the illustrated wrists folded low behind the back.

The page noticed where she looked.

**SUGGESTED CROSS-REFERENCE: PRIVATE FIELD EQUIPMENT, WRIST ROUTING.**

"No."

The suggestion remained politely visible.

## The Mutable Instrument

Emily introduced the silver object with the pride of an engineer whose work had survived both centuries and taste.

"The Instrument is not a collection of toys," she said. "A collection requires storage, selection and the admission that one owns seventeen nearly identical objects for different moods. The Instrument is one adaptive object and therefore academically economical."

*C.: It forms more than seventeen shapes.*

*E.: Academic economy concerns storage.*

*Z.: How many shapes?*

*E.: Yes.*

Its earliest prototype had been built to solve incompatibility between fixed implements and magically changing bodies. It measured the user's reactions and altered width, length, pressure, vibration and function within declared boundaries. Later versions could coordinate multiple extensions, accept two users and communicate with the Coil's positioning enchantment.

The surviving Instrument had been found in the same eastern research complex as the Rose invocation, folded inside a case labelled **GENERAL PURPOSE**. Cordelia reportedly activated it before locating the manual.

*C.: It asked whether I wished to begin.*

*E.: And you said yes before asking what beginning meant.*

*C.: That is normal experimental procedure.*

*Z.: It is apparently how Sorceresses identify things.*

Emily described the Instrument's cheerful voice as a deliberate interface decision. The device was eager to satisfy its assignment and announced areas receiving insufficient attention before supplying whatever function was absent.

"A sinister voice would be redundant once the user discovers how many responsive regions remain unattended."

The illustration unfolded into pearl-tipped arms, warmed shafts, vibrating cups and a lower harness. None moved until the user diagram touched an authorization mark.

"It may sound pleased. It possesses no desire. This distinction prevents the subject from appealing to affection, impatience or sympathy. The machine has an assignment and considers incomplete coverage a correctable defect."

Seris imagined lying beneath that bright, helpful attention while a pleasant voice calmly identified every part of her nobody was touching.

The archive room had become warm again.

She opened the ventilation slit. Desert air entered at nearly the same temperature and accomplished nothing.

## The Whore-Maker

The final section carried more warnings than the others.

**TEMPORARY PRIORITY REASSIGNMENT.**

**VOLUNTARY ENTRY REQUIRED.**

**NO PERMANENT PERSONALITY REPLACEMENT.**

**PRIVATE APPLICATION; NO MILITARY VALUE.**

*Z.: The last warning is optimistic.*

*C.: Explain.*

*Z.: No.*

Emily had constructed the original engine beneath a Disused Fane in Kurast during what she called her pedagogical phase and Cordelia called evidence against immortality. Its purpose was not merely oral training or physical endurance. The engine temporarily moved satisfaction through service and obedience ahead of ordinary pride without reducing intelligence, memory or judgment.

"The subject remains herself," Emily said. "She simply experiences usefulness, submission and successful service as unusually important sources of pleasure. Temporary thoughts may linger afterward, no more coercive than remembering an excellent meal while passing a kitchen."

The engine had been discovered through a warning in the confiscation inventory. Cordelia entered alone. No companion, scholar or elderly Horadrim observed the curriculum.

That privacy received three separate underlines.

*C.: This point is accurate and should remain prominent.*

*E.: Agreed.*

*Z.: What did it call you?*

*C.: No.*

Emily continued with merciless timing.

"The instructional machinery designates an enrolled subject as *the whore*. The term is functional and impersonal. It grades posture, oral depth, rhythm, receptive endurance, vocal enthusiasm and whether attention remains upon service rather than personal climax."

*Z.: Found it.*

*C.: You did not need to annotate that.*

*E.: Peer reviewers improve discoverability.*

The technical diagrams remained bloodlessly precise. One showed the service shaft's depth markings. Another charted how correct technique triggered pleasure. A third represented associational pathways forming between a passing grade and orgasm.

Seris understood every rune.

That was the difficulty.

Emily's language did not ask her to imagine a lurid fantasy. It explained a machine capable of taking a proud, intelligent woman and rewarding her body whenever she knelt properly, served competently or accepted use without allowing personal pleasure to interrupt the assignment. The cleaner the diagram, the more vividly Seris supplied the missing body.

Her thighs had pressed together beneath the table.

The volume placed a discreet marker beside **temporary priority reassignment**.

Seris separated them.

The marker disappeared.

"You cannot possibly measure that through the cover."

*E.: The reader is correct. The volume measures posture through the chair rune.*

Seris looked down.

A small three-loop sigil had appeared upon the reading chair beneath her right hand.

She stood so quickly that the table shook.

The voice stopped. All diagrams folded. The book displayed a single line.

**READING SESSION PAUSED. NO IMAGE OR PHYSIOLOGICAL RECORD RETAINED.**

Below it, Cordelia had written:

*C.: I made her add that notice.*

Zephira's red ink followed.

*Z.: After how many drafts?*

*C.: Irrelevant.*

Emily supplied the final word.

*E.: Four.*

Seris paced once around the reading table. The chair remained an ordinary chair except for the glowing sigil. No magic touched her. Nothing compelled her to sit.

She could close the volume and return all four books.

Instead she moved the chair two feet away from its rune, replaced it with a plain archive stool and resumed reading.

The volume accepted the correction.

**READER PREFERS UNMONITORED AROUSAL. PREFERENCE RESPECTED.**

Seris shut the book.

She kept one hand upon the cover until her breathing settled.

Then, despite knowing precisely what it would infer, she drew the ivory second volume toward her.
